# PRD Quality Review — querbeet

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely good PRD: the thesis is stated and bet on ("the pipeline itself is a portable artifact… the author's expertise travels; the data does not", §1), the characteristic failure mode is named once and then drives roughly a dozen FRs (§4 preamble, "a plausible wrong number, not an error message"), and the consequence bullets are unusually testable for a document at this stage. What is at risk is not the argument but its bookkeeping: §6.3's largest-single-risk paragraph and Open Question 1's build gate are both stale or in conflict with the scope §6.1 declares, and §0 promises the reader two open questions in §8 when only one of them is there. The document's own conventions — the `[ASSUMPTION]` roundtrip, the tech-stays-in-the-addendum rule — are asserted in §0 and then not kept, which is the sort of drift that costs a downstream workflow more than a missing section would.

## 1. Decision-readiness — adequate

Trade-offs are named with what was given up, which is the hard part and this PRD does it repeatedly: §6.2 cuts the interactive HTML export and says what it costs the Consumer; §6.2 defers roles and permissions and flags it as "the item most likely to be needed sooner than planned"; SM-C1 explicitly forbids optimising the metric the rest of the document would otherwise reward. FR-25's measured-constraint block is a model of surfacing an unwelcome finding rather than smoothing it — it states that the `file://` bucket is shared, that two copies of the tool collide, and that this "cannot be fixed from inside querbeet", then turns the finding into a disclosure requirement instead of pretending it is solved. NFR-8 is amended in place with a dated qualification rather than quietly rewritten. A reader pushing on data isolation finds their objection acknowledged.

Two things pull this below strong. §6.3 — the section whose whole job is to tell the owner where the scope risk is — is arguing against a state of the world that no longer exists. And Open Question 1 issues a build-order instruction that §6.1 contradicts, with neither section noticing.

The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at real tensions (FR-9's friction-versus-correctness gate, the Consumer-view deferral, the four-surface scope observation), not at safe checkpoints. §8's entries, where they are genuinely open, carry owners and resolution costs — Q1 names the cheapest resolution and why it is blocked, Q6 names an owner and a date.

### Findings

- **high** Open Question 1's build gate contradicts §6.1's declared MVP scope (§8 Q1 vs §6.1) — Q1 concludes "Nothing Consumer-specific should be built before it closes; everything Author-facing can proceed", and in the same breath records that Christian "is not reachable in the near term", so the gate has no expected close date. But five in-scope FRs realize UJ-2 and nothing else — FR-11, FR-23, FR-24, FR-32, FR-33 — and FR-22 and FR-34 realize only UJ-2 and UJ-4. Taken literally the gate freezes the Pre-flight/mapping path and the Package, which §6.1 lists as MVP. §6.3's third observation ("the natural build order is the risk order… a version that replaces the PowerQuery workflow end to end and nothing more") is probably the intended reconciliation, but it is never connected to Q1 and does not use the same words. *Fix:* either narrow Q1's gate to the things that actually depend on Christian's answer (Package shape, Recipe distribution mechanism, Consumer-only view) and say the Pre-flight path proceeds regardless, or make Q1 point at §6.3's build order as the resolution.
- **high** §6.3's second observation is stale and its cross-reference is wrong (§6.3) — "the graph Editor is the largest single risk and it arrived late… it is the one component in the entire stack for which no technology research exists. See Open Questions 4 and 9." All three clauses have been overtaken: R6 screened the field and a spike built and measured the Editor (addendum §7 — anchor drift 0 px / 0.02 px, cycle guard on all three paths, no Editor/table contention across 2,800 window swaps, Recipe round-tripping byte-identically); OQ 4 records that "R2's Vue 3 verdict survived"; and the cited Open Questions 4 and 9 are both closed, with 9 being about `idea.md`/`README.md` contradictions and having nothing to do with the Editor. The correct pointers are Questions 2 and 3, as the addendum's own "Pipeline shape" note says ("recorded in PRD §6.3 and Open Questions 2–4"). The effect is that the section a reader consults to learn where the danger is nominates a risk that has since been measured down, and points them at two struck-through entries. *Fix:* rewrite the paragraph against R6 and the spike, repoint to Questions 2 and 3, and re-rank — on current evidence the LLM correction loop (never exercised, per Q3) and the Package container (unexamined, per Q8) are ahead of the Editor.
- **medium** §8 is doing two jobs and the open half is hard to find (§8) — four of ten entries are struck-through research history (2, 4, 7, 9), and entry 3 wraps roughly four hundred words of *answer* around one residual open item, the correction loop that "has never run". A reader scanning §8 for "what is still undecided" has to parse prose to extract six live items. The content is valuable and should not be deleted. *Fix:* split into "Open" (1, 3-residual, 5, 6, 8, 10) and "Closed, kept for the record", and reduce entry 3's open half to the one sentence that is still a question.

## 2. Substance over theater — strong

No furniture found. Three personas, one of which (the Boxchecker) "never opens querbeet" and is explicitly credited with shaping exactly one requirement — "They shape one requirement rather than many" (§2) — which is the opposite of persona theater; a fourth persona was not invented to fill a table. The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD: the Probe Query mechanism ("The model comes to *understand* the data without ever *seeing* it") and the claim that provable network silence removes an approval process are specific to this product. The NFRs carry product-specific numbers rather than adjectives — NFR-3 gives 100,000 per Source and half a million total, NFR-4 names lead and secondary browsers with a decision date and a measured consequence (the Firefox spacer collapse at ~614,000 rows), NFR-2 is stated as "verifiable from the built artifact". NFR-7 is a non-goal that still carries one binding rule, and says why that rule is a correctness rule rather than an accessibility one.

The §4 preamble earns its place hardest: rather than sprinkling defensiveness through the FRs, it states the failure mode once and each of FR-9, FR-13, FR-14, FR-15, FR-34 then references a measured behaviour of a real library. No findings.

## 3. Strategic coherence — strong

There is a thesis and the document bets on it. §1 states it, §2 shapes the roles around the handoff it implies, SM-2 tests it directly and names the failure condition out loud ("if it fails, the product is a good personal utility and the Recipe concept was wrong"), and §5's "Deferred, not rejected" block draws the boundary against self-service BI on operating model rather than feature list — "It is file in, file out. A department that outgrows that has outgrown querbeet, and that is the correct outcome." That is a thesis defended, not a category disclaimer. Counter-metrics exist and both counterbalance a named primary. The MVP scope kind is coherent (problem-solving, with the portability bet layered on) and §6.3 admits the tension between the two rather than papering it: only the transformation path has an existing workflow behind it.

Two seams. The metric set leaves an entire feature group unvalidated, and the Package is the one artifact in scope with no journey behind it.

### Findings

- **medium** No success metric validates FR-31 – FR-35 (§7) — SM-1 covers FR-1 – FR-19 plus FR-36, FR-37; SM-2 covers FR-20 – FR-25; SM-3 covers FR-21 – FR-23, FR-25; SM-4 covers FR-26 – FR-30. The Result table, view filters, full-dataset search, run status and the whole tile Dashboard are validated by nothing — and §6.3 independently identifies the Dashboard as the weakest-evidenced surface, "evidenced by one named need rather than by a practice". So the least-supported block of the MVP is also the unmeasured one. *Fix:* either extend SM-1 to FR-31 – FR-35 (the patch-compliance run does land on the Result table and does export a Dashboard), or state in §7 that the presentation layer deliberately carries no metric and why.
- **medium** The Package (FR-24) has a glossary entry, an MVP slot and an open container question, but no journey (§3, §4.3, §6.1, §8 Q8) — FR-24 says "Realizes UJ-2", yet UJ-2 has Christian receiving "a Recipe from Ben and his four export files", which is the Recipe path, not the Package path. No UJ shows anyone sending or opening a Package; SM-2's wording — "without the Author touching that data" — describes the Recipe handoff specifically and reads as an odd fit for an artifact whose whole point is that it contains data. Meanwhile Q8 concedes the mechanism and its cost are "unexamined". This is the one place where the FR list runs ahead of the evidence. *Fix:* add a two-line journey beat showing who sends a Package to whom and why the Recipe would not have done, or move FR-24 to §6.2 until Q8 closes.

## 4. Done-ness clarity — adequate

The per-FR consequence bullets are the strongest thing in the document for downstream use. Most are genuinely testable and several are testable in a way that would catch the exact bug they exist to prevent: FR-13's "The output row count equals the sum of the input row counts", FR-9's worked example "Date, dd.mm.yyyy — 842 of 900 values readable" and its explicit distinction between *decisive evidence* and *nothing settles it*, FR-15's "held in the Recipe as a number in canonical machine form — `1000`, never `"1.000"` and never `"1000"`", FR-36's "UTF-8 with a byte-order mark and a configurable delimiter defaulting to semicolon", FR-37's "21–35 of the application's own formatted axis labels as text and… zero raster images". FR-28 even records a requirement that was *loosened* after a spike showed it would be stricter than the Editor, with the reasoning kept. An engineer can write tests from these.

The gap is not in the individual bullets, it is that one thing nothing describes — when the Pipeline runs — and a handful of performance adjectives in a document that everywhere else prefers numbers.

### Findings

- **high** No FR owns Pipeline execution, and the document implies two incompatible models (§4.2, §4.3, §4.5) — FR-19 says "Changing a Step's configuration updates its Preview and the Preview of every Step downstream of it", which is eager recomputation; UJ-2 says "He runs the Pipeline and lands on the Dashboard", FR-9 says "Running the Pipeline is blocked until confirmation", FR-22 says "The Pipeline does not execute while any requirement is missing", and FR-34 reports on "the run that produced it", all of which imply a discrete, user-triggered run. There is no FR describing what triggers execution, whether the Result and Dashboard recompute on every edit, or what "a run" is for the purposes of FR-34's status. This is the single largest hole for story creation: it sits underneath FR-19, FR-22, FR-31, FR-34 and FR-35, and every one of them has to assume an answer. *Fix:* add one FR to §4.2 defining the execution model — trigger, what is recomputed, what invalidates a run status — and make FR-34's "run" refer to it.
- **medium** Four performance requirements are stated as adjectives in a PRD that has the measurements (FR-8, FR-31, FR-33, FR-36) — "The Preview renders within an interaction-responsive time regardless of Source size" (FR-8), "Scrolling through a hundred thousand rows stays smooth" (FR-31), "Searching a hundred thousand rows returns within an interaction-responsive time" (FR-33), "An export that takes noticeably long shows progress" (FR-36). These are the rubric's flagged phrases, and they are avoidable here: the research measured 50-row window swaps at 2.9–5 ms and exports at 4.9–30.6 s, so a threshold is available rather than invented. *Fix:* define one budget in NFR-3 — e.g. "interactive operations complete within 100 ms; anything above 1 s shows progress" — and have FR-8, FR-31, FR-33 and FR-36 reference it instead of each coining a phrase.
- **medium** FR-9's full-column scan carries no time budget and no progress requirement (§4.1 FR-9) — the FR mandates "Detection reads every value in the column, not a sample" and argues the case well, but says nothing about what that costs or what the user sees while it happens. The addendum identifies this exact loop as the per-cell hot path — "Budget type detection as rows × columns × candidates tried, not rows × columns", with the one measured data point (Luxon) at roughly 7 s for a 100k × 20 Source. FR-36's much cheaper exports get a progress requirement; FR-9's scan gets none, and it runs at file-load time on the path every user takes. *Fix:* add a consequence bounding detection time at the NFR-3 target, or requiring progress and non-blocking behaviour above a stated duration.
- **low** FR-29's large-result threshold is not a threshold (§4.4 FR-29) — "A Probe Query whose result would be large — more rows than a summary — is reported as such." "More rows than a summary" is not testable. *Fix:* name a row count.
- **low** FR-35's "same small form" does not fit two of its five tile kinds (§4.5 FR-35) — "Every tile is configured through the same small form: grouping column, measured column, aggregation, row limit." A key-figure tile has no grouping column and no row limit; a table tile has no measured column or aggregation. *Fix:* say which fields each tile kind uses, or soften to "the same form, with fields that do not apply to a tile kind hidden".
- **low** Nothing requires a long operation to be cancellable — R4 measured cancellation deliberately (message-queue latency 2–3 ms, progress at ~2.6 % overhead, and the finding that `SharedArrayBuffer` is unavailable from `file://`), which implies someone expected a cancel affordance, but no FR asks for one. FR-36 requires progress and a responsive interface, not abort. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-36, or state in §6.2 that cancellation is out of MVP scope.

## 5. Scope honesty — strong

This is the document's best dimension after the FR consequences. §5 separates permanent non-goals from MVP deferrals and says why the separation matters — "a downstream reader treating a deferral as a principle will build the wrong thing" — which is precisely the failure that section exists to prevent. §6.2 lists seven exclusions with reasons, including the ones a reader might otherwise assume present (multiple Result Steps, legacy Excel formats). De-scoping is done out loud: the interactive HTML export is cut in §6.2 *and* flagged under FR-37 *and* recorded in addendum §6. §6.3 volunteers that the MVP is "a substantial build for one person" and declines to turn that into a recommendation, which is the honest move for a PRD whose reader is also its author.

UJ-2's disclosure is handled about as well as a hypothesis can be: tagged at the heading, called out in §2.3's preamble as load-bearing ("**UJ-1, UJ-3 and UJ-4 describe work the Author does today and were captured from him. UJ-2 is a hypothesis**"), given its own "What is unknown and must be validated" bullet inside the journey, indexed in §9, and owned by Open Question 1. Four independent markers, none of them buried. The only problem it creates is the scope collision reported under Decision-readiness.

Open-items density — 6 live Open Questions, 8 indexed assumptions, 4 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts — is entirely appropriate for a personal tool that is explicitly not a launch document.

### Findings

- **high** §0 promises two open questions in §8 and only one is there (§0 vs §8) — "Two questions are still open at the time of writing — how the view document of FR-37 is produced, and what container FR-24's Package uses — and both are named in §8." The Package container is Question 8. There is no entry anywhere in §8 about how the FR-37 HTML and PDF documents are produced; the only record of that gap is in the addendum's currency note ("**R8 (view document export) has not run**"). A reader who trusts §0 and reads §8 concludes the view-document question was answered. Since FR-37 is the Boxchecker's entire reason to exist and carries the most specific measured requirement in the document (vector charts with selectable axis text), the missing entry hides the least-supported requirement in §4.6. *Fix:* add Open Question 11 naming R8, what it must decide (print pipeline for the PDF, embedding strategy for the HTML), and that FR-37's vector-text consequence currently rests on a chart-renderer measurement rather than on an export mechanism.
- **medium** The `[ASSUMPTION]` roundtrip is broken in the direction §0 rules out (§0, §9) — §0 states the convention: "Inferences that were not confirmed carry an inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag and are indexed in §9." §9 lists eight assumptions; only two of them exist inline — UJ-2's heading (§2.3) and SM-2 (§7). The other six have index entries but no tag at the location they point to: the Boxchecker's needs (§2), Aggregate as a sixth Step kind (FR-18), exactly one Result Step (FR-12), that locale ambiguity can be surfaced usefully (FR-9), that a static view document suffices (FR-37), and that the transformation path is separable (§6.3). Every one of these reads as settled where it appears; a reader working from the FR alone never learns it is an inference. The FR-12 one is the costliest, since single-Result-Step is called out in §6.2 as a deliberate exclusion and the assumption behind it — "was not requested either way" — never surfaces there. *Fix:* add the six inline tags, or amend §0 to say the index is the authoritative record and inline tags are used only where the assumption is the whole point.

## 6. Downstream usability — adequate

This PRD is explicitly chain-top — §0 names architecture, UX and epic breakdown as consumers — so this dimension carries real weight, and the ID hygiene is good. FR-1 through FR-37 are contiguous with no gaps and no duplicates; §6.1's six scope bullets cover all 37 with no overlap and no omission; NFR-1 through NFR-8 are all defined; UJ-1 through UJ-4 are all defined and every `Realizes UJ-n` resolves. The Glossary is present, has a German UI column that downstream UX work will need, and its terms are used verbatim in the FRs rather than paraphrased. Sections mostly stand alone: FR-14 carries its own reasoning about duplicate keys without requiring the reader to have read §4's preamble.

Two things hold it at adequate: the broken assumptions roundtrip reported above (§9 is the section a downstream workflow would source-extract from, and six of its eight entries have no inline anchor to extract *to*), and one wrong cross-reference in §8 that would send a reader to the wrong FR. The remaining drift is small and listed under Mechanical notes.

### Findings

- **low** §0's own separation rule is broken by three requirements (§0 vs FR-24, FR-25, NFR-4) — "This PRD states *capabilities*; the technology decisions and their consequences are recorded in `addendum.md`." FR-24's last consequence reasons about "the database or key name"; FR-25's measured-constraint block names IndexedDB, the `file://` origin and `navigator.storage.persist()` and specifies "Startup never blocks on `navigator.storage.persist()`"; NFR-4 specifies the spacer clamping behaviour of two engines. The content is right to be in the PRD — each of these is a user-visible constraint, not an implementation detail — but the rule as written in §0 is absolute and these break it, which will confuse anyone deciding where to put the next such finding. *Fix:* soften §0 to "technology *choices* live in the addendum; a measured platform constraint that changes what the user sees is stated here", which is what the document actually does.

## 7. Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is a solo internal tool, but it is not a single-operator tool — the entire thesis is a handoff between two roles, so UJs are load-bearing rather than overhead, and there are four rather than the eight a template would have invited. §7 says its own rigor level out loud: "Stakes are personal and internal, so these are deliberately coarse. They exist to prevent optimising the wrong thing, not to be reported to anyone." The metrics that follow are operational and binary — a workflow retired, a Recipe run once by someone else — which is right for the stakes and would be wrong for a launch. Non-goals are used where they do real work rather than as a formality. Nothing here is over-formalised, and the one place a lighter document would have under-formalised — the Recipe format contract that a language model has to satisfy — gets the most rigor in the document (FR-20, FR-28, Open Question 3).

### Findings

- **low** UJ-4 has no named protagonist (§2.3 UJ-4) — "**Persona + context:** Either operating role, repeating a known analysis against fresh files." UJ-1, UJ-2 and UJ-3 name Ben or Christian and carry their context inline; UJ-4 floats. It is also the journey SM-3 measures ("takes under five minutes"), so the one journey with a numeric target is the one with no person in it. *Fix:* name Christian doing the monthly run — it is the same person as UJ-2 one month later — or say explicitly that UJ-4 is deliberately role-agnostic because both roles perform it identically.
